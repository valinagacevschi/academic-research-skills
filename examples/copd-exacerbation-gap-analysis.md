# COPD exacerbations: research gap analysis (state as of 11 October 2026)

Scope: acute exacerbations of COPD (ECOPD): definition, acute treatment, prevention, the post-exacerbation period, and the Romanian setting. This is a rapid evidence scan, not a systematic review. Sources marked † were read only as search abstracts or snippets, not as full text. Check them before citing them in a manuscript.

## Bottom line

The field has moved fast on **prevention with biologics** and on **recognising the post-exacerbation cardiopulmonary risk**. Acute-phase care has barely changed: steroids, antibiotics and the definition itself. Six gaps look most researchable now, the first three especially from a Romanian base:

1. **Validation of the Rome severity classification** in real-world and Eastern European cohorts, and its uptake as a trial endpoint.
2. **Biomarker-guided acute treatment** (eosinophil-guided steroids, CRP-guided antibiotics) moving from UK/European primary-care RCTs to hospital care and to health systems like Romania's.
3. **The 30–180-day post-exacerbation window.** Cardiovascular risk stays high for months[12], but no trial tests an intervention aimed at it.
4. **Biologic positioning.** Head-to-head comparisons, use in eosinophil-low patients, and who should get which drug; four drug classes now compete.
5. **Vaccination (RSV) as exacerbation prevention.** The only dedicated RCT starts in 2027[16].
6. **Romanian real-world data.** No published Romanian cohort on ECOPD burden, readmission or the Rome classification was found.

## Gap map

| # | Domain | What is established | What is missing (the gap) | Evidence status | Feasible study design |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Definition & severity | Rome proposal (2021) defines ECOPD with objective severity variables[1]. Validation cohorts exist (Maastricht, Korea)[2]†. A 2025 AJRCCM viewpoint says it is "time to move" to it[2]†. | Not validated in Central/Eastern European cohorts or primary care. Unclear how it performs as a **trial endpoint**, and against older (Anthonisen/GOLD) severity in already-published trials. | Moderate: several retrospective validations, no prospective multinational one | Retrospective multicentre Romanian hospital cohort: Rome vs GOLD severity vs in-hospital death, NIV, ICU, 90-day readmission |
| 2 | GOLD 2026 risk stratification | GOLD 2026 lowered Group E to ≥1 moderate exacerbation and added a "disease activity" concept[3]. | Whether the lower threshold over-treats; first EHR analyses only now appearing[4]†. No prospective data on "low disease activity" as a treatment target. | Early | EHR cohort: outcomes of patients reclassified to E by the 2026 rule |
| 3 | Acute steroids | Eosinophil-guided prednisolone was non-inferior to standard steroids in UK primary care (STARR2)[5]. Benralizumab at exacerbation onset was tested in ABRA[6]. | **Hospitalised** ECOPD: no adequately powered eosinophil-guided RCT. No data on implementation (point-of-care eosinophils), and none outside the UK. Optimal dose/duration in eosinophil-low patients. | Primary care: good. Hospital: thin. | Pragmatic hospital RCT or stepped-wedge implementation study |
| 4 | Antibiotics | Point-of-care CRP reduced antibiotic use without harm in UK primary care (PACE)[15]†. | Uptake outside the UK. Hospital setting. Combined CRP plus eosinophil "treat-the-trait" algorithm. Antimicrobial-resistance outcomes. | Primary care: good. Elsewhere: thin. | Cluster-RCT or interrupted time series of CRP point-of-care testing in Romanian primary care / emergency departments |
| 5 | Biologics for prevention | Dupilumab (BOREAS/NOTUS) and mepolizumab (MATINEE) reduce exacerbations in eosinophilic COPD[7]†[8]†. Tozorakimab (anti-IL-33) cut moderate/severe exacerbations by 29–34% in OBERON/TITANIA, **including patients with eosinophils <150 (−23%)**; US filing under priority review[9]. Itepekimab: AERIFY-1 positive (−27%), AERIFY-2 negative[10]. Astegolimab: phase IIb ALIENTO positive, phase III ARNASA negative[11]. | **No head-to-head trials.** Only indirect comparisons[8]†. Inconsistent anti-IL-33/ST2 results across replicate trials are unexplained (population? endpoint? smoking status?). No biomarker yet predicts response in eosinophil-low patients. No data on stopping or switching, or cost-effectiveness in middle-income systems. | Rapidly evolving | Network meta-analysis once OBERON/TITANIA are fully published. Pooled responder analysis. Romanian real-world dupilumab registry (CNAS protocol already defines eligibility). |
| 6 | Post-exacerbation cardiopulmonary risk | EXACOS-CV meta-analysis: severe cardiovascular events or death HR 10.22 (days 1–7). Risk stays raised up to 6 months after a moderate and 1 year after a severe exacerbation[12]. | **No intervention trial** targets this window: cardiology review, beta-blocker or statin optimisation, troponin/NT-proBNP screening at discharge. Mechanisms poorly characterised. Data beyond 1 year come from only 3 countries[12]. | Risk: strong. Intervention: absent. | Discharge cardiopulmonary bundle RCT. Biomarker cohort (troponin, NT-proBNP) at admission and 30/90 days. |
| 7 | Readmission & mortality after hospitalisation | Global meta-analysis of 65,945 patients quantifies readmission and mortality[13]†. Pulmonary rehab after exacerbation reduces readmission[14]†. | Rehab **uptake** stays low, and barriers are documented but rarely tested[14]†. Discharge bundles vary, and no core bundle has been shown to work. Eastern European data are absent. | Moderate | Hybrid implementation trial of early or home-based rehab. Romanian readmission cohort. |
| 8 | Vaccination | RSV is common in ECOPD; observational data suggest the adjuvanted RSVPreF3 vaccine reduces severe ECOPD and cardiovascular events[17]†. | No completed RCT with **exacerbation** as the primary endpoint. The first (NCT07508644, n=320, open-label) starts July 2027, primary completion 2030[16]. | Weak | Test-negative or target-trial-emulation study using registry data |
| 9 | Prediction & remote monitoring | Many machine-learning and remote-monitoring prediction models[18]†. | Few externally validated. Almost none tested for **impact** (does an alert change outcomes?). Data and equity gaps. Little evidence on clinician workload. | Weak on impact | External validation on a different population, then a randomised impact study |
| 10 | Romania-specific | 2026 national COPD guideline published (CMR)[20]. TB history is linked to earlier, more severe COPD and earlier first severe exacerbation in a Romanian multicentre study[19]†. | **No Romanian data found** on ECOPD incidence, readmission, Rome severity, rehab uptake, or real-world biologic use. EARCO Romanian participation unverified (repo research note). Post-TB COPD exacerbation phenotype barely studied. | Very weak | DRG/hospital discharge-data cohort. Post-TB COPD exacerbation cohort. |

## Caveats

- Rapid scan, single reviewer, about 20 sources. Not exhaustive, no risk-of-bias assessment.
- The tozorakimab figures come from the sponsor's press release (8 September 2026)[9]. Check them against the NEJM papers before citing.
- Items marked † were seen only as search abstracts or snippets. ERS, Lancet and Taylor & Francis full texts were blocked for automated retrieval.
- "No Romanian data found" means none turned up in this search, not that none exist. Run a PubMed/Scopus search with Romanian affiliation filters before claiming novelty.
- This is a research-planning document, not clinical guidance.
